CostLens

AI-powered Manufacturing Cost Intelligence

A product concept for helping manufacturing and cost teams understand what changed in their operations, why costs or WIP moved, where the largest drivers came from, and what to investigate next.

01 · THE PRODUCT PROBLEM

Enterprise systems capture the answer. Users still have to find the story.

Manufacturing and supply chain systems record thousands of transactions across materials, work orders, operations, labor, machine usage, overhead, inventory and accounting. The information exists, but answering a business question often means stitching together multiple views before a user can explain what happened.

Primary userCost / manufacturing analyst

Investigates period-end exceptions and explains cost movement.

Secondary userPlant / operations manager

Needs a concise explanation of operational and cost movement.

Core jobExplain the outcome

Move from an unexpected result to evidence, drivers and next action.

Product insight: The gap is not simply “access to data.” The gap is the ability to turn connected operational data into an explanation a person can act on.

02 · THE JOBS TO BE DONE

Questions users ask at the end of a manufacturing period.

01

“Why is this work order 18% above expected cost?”
Trace the variance from total cost to the largest component and underlying transactions.

02

“Why is WIP unusually high this month?”
Connect WIP balances to open work orders, aging, accumulated costs and production status.

03

“How has the cost of this product changed over time?”
Compare periods, identify material/labor/overhead drivers and explain the trend.

04

“What needs my attention before period close?”
Surface unusual variances, aged WIP, incomplete production and other investigation candidates.

03 · PRODUCT THESIS

Don't build a chatbot. Build an investigation workflow.

The AI is valuable because it can orchestrate multiple steps of analysis and turn the results into a coherent explanation. It should not replace deterministic business calculations or simply summarize whatever happens to be retrieved.

Design decision

Calculate first, explain second

Financial and operational metrics come from deterministic calculations. The AI interprets and connects those results.

Design decision

Trace before summarizing

Start from the outcome and move down to the drivers rather than generating a generic narrative.

Design decision

Show the evidence chain

Make it clear which facts were observed, which metrics were derived, and which explanations are hypotheses.

Design decision

Recommend the next check

Finish with a concrete investigative action instead of stopping at a description of the problem.

04 · THE EDGE CASES

Where the product has to be careful.

A manufacturing explanation can be confidently wrong if the system treats incomplete data as a complete story. These are first-class product requirements, not afterthoughts.

Incomplete period data

Clearly indicate when transactions are still processing or the period is not yet complete.

Multiple plausible drivers

Rank evidence-backed contributors rather than forcing a single root cause.

Timing effects

Separate a real economic change from a transaction, receipt or valuation timing difference.

Low-volume anomalies

A large percentage change on a tiny base should not automatically become a high-priority alert.

Hierarchy confusion

Keep plant, product, work order, operation and cost-component levels explicit.

Missing evidence

When the available data cannot support the explanation, the product should say so and identify the missing evidence.

05 · SUCCESS METRICS

Measure whether investigation becomes faster and more trustworthy.

Time to explanation

How long does it take a user to move from an exception to a credible explanation?

Investigation completion

How often can users answer the question without switching across multiple systems?

Evidence coverage

What share of material claims in an answer can be traced back to supporting data?

Useful abstention

How often does the system correctly avoid over-claiming when evidence is incomplete?

06 · SYSTEM DESIGN

Use AI for orchestration and explanation — not for arithmetic.

The architecture keeps business logic and generative reasoning separate. That makes the product easier to evaluate, debug and extend.

01User question

Natural-language investigation request.

02Intent + tools

Route to cost, WIP, trend and transaction analysis.

03Evidence layer

Combine deterministic metrics with contextual retrieval.

04Explanation

Generate drivers, evidence and next investigative steps.

07 · AI EVALUATION

Trust has to be designed into the product.

The evaluation plan focuses on the things that matter for this workflow rather than generic language-model quality.

Factual accuracy

Do calculated values and retrieved facts match the underlying records?

Driver accuracy

Does the answer identify the right contributors without inventing causes?

Evidence traceability

Can material claims be tied to specific records or documentation?

Failure behavior

Does the system qualify or abstain when evidence is missing or contradictory?

08 · BUILD ROADMAP

PRODUCT ROLE

How I approached the problem

I treated the opportunity as a workflow and decision problem first, then worked backward into the AI architecture.

  • Define users and high-value investigation moments
  • Map the data needed to answer each question
  • Separate deterministic calculations from generative reasoning
  • Design evidence, uncertainty and failure behavior
  • Define an evaluation plan before scaling the experience
THE CORE INSIGHT

“The product shouldn't tell users what the data says. It should help them understand what the data means.”

DOMAIN CONTEXT

Why this is a meaningful AI problem

Manufacturing cost outcomes are the result of many connected events. The product opportunity is to make those connections understandable without hiding the underlying evidence.
